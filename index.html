ArkAide

Bible story games for little ones, for iPad. Noah's Ark, the Wise Builder, the Lost Sheep, David and Goliath, the Good Shepherd, and Nehemiah's Wall, with skill levels that grow with your child, and your own family's voices.

Questions or ideas?

Email support@thearkaide.com. It's a small family project, so it may take a day or two to answer, but every message is read.

Getting started

  • The grown-ups area: on the home screen, press and hold the gear for a moment, then answer the math question. That's where you add children, set skill levels, record voices, choose the narrator, and invite family.
  • Skill levels: Little one (every tap is a win, about ages 2–3), Big kid (find, count, and aim, with help after a few misses, about ages 4–5), and Challenge (faster, no helpers, about 6 and up). Move a child up as they grow.
  • Guest play: kids can play without a name. The Guest card has its own skill level and stars.
  • Leaving a game: press and hold the house button for a moment, so little fingers don't leave by accident.

How the games work

Every game tells a Bible story, read aloud, with the verse on screen. Each child plays at their own skill level, and the games change to match. The same explanations are in the app under About → How the games work.

🚢 Noah's Ark

Genesis 6–9: God saves Noah, his family, and the animals, and gives the rainbow as a promise.

Balloons float across the sky carrying animals. Tap a balloon to pop it, and the animal goes into the ark.

When the ark is full, the story finishes: God shuts the door, everyone counts the animals, it rains for forty days, the dove brings back a leaf, and the rainbow appears.

  • Little one: Pop any balloon. Every pop is a win, and the animal says its name and sound. Twelve animals fill the ark.
  • Big kid: Matching and counting: "Find 2 lions!" A picture shows what to look for, and a wrong animal just wiggles and says hi. Six pairs fill the ark.
  • Challenge: The same, with faster, smaller balloons and no hints.
  • Tip: A sparkly rainbow balloon floats by now and then; popping it sets off a surprise.
  • Tip: Tap the animals in the ark's windows to hear them again.

🏠 The Wise Builder

Matthew 7:24–27: Jesus' story of the wise man who built on the rock and the foolish man who built on the sand.

Friendly trucks drive by carrying house pieces. Tap a truck and its piece flies onto the house on the rock.

When the house is done, the storm comes: the house on the sand falls, and the house on the rock stands firm.

  • Little one: Tap any truck. The house goes up piece by piece: floor, walls, door, window, roof, and a few extras.
  • Big kid: Shapes: "Find the triangle for the roof!" "Find 2 squares!" A wrong truck just honks and says what shape it has.
  • Challenge: The same shapes, with trucks that come more often and drive faster.
  • Tip: It's a quick game, good for the youngest players and for learning shape names.

🐑 The Lost Sheep

Luke 15:3–7: the shepherd leaves the ninety-nine to find the one lost sheep.

Count the sheep in the pen by tapping each one. One little lamb is missing!

Search the meadow's hiding places (rocks, a haystack, a bush, a log, flowers). Each one hides a surprise animal or the lamb. The shepherd carries the lamb home, and everyone celebrates.

  • Little one: Peekaboo: tap any hiding place. After a few misses, the lamb's hiding place starts to wiggle.
  • Big kid: Listen for the clue ("a baa behind something YELLOW," "behind the LITTLE rock") to practice colors and size words. Tap ð to hear it again.
  • Challenge: The same clues, with smaller places to tap.
  • Tip: Good for listening and colors; let your child say the clue back to you.

🎯 David & Goliath

1 Samuel 17: young David trusts God and faces the giant with a sling and five smooth stones.

Four short chapters: David practices with his sling while guarding the sheep, tries on King Saul's too-big armor, picks five smooth stones from the brook, and faces Goliath.

Goliath's mean words appear as dark clouds; tap them into sunshine. Then one stone sends the giant toppling, dizzy (nobody gets hurt).

Sling Practice (the middle card on the menu) is a slingshot physics puzzle: pull back and let go to knock over towers of wood and stone and send a lion, a bear, and a giant running. Fewer stones earn more stars, and ↻ retries a level.

  • Little one: Tap anywhere to throw; the stone is aimed for them.
  • Big kid: Pull back to aim, with a dotted arc showing where the stone will go, and let go to throw. After a few misses, the arc shows the whole path.
  • Challenge: A shorter aiming arc that never shows the whole path, and smaller targets.
  • Tip: Sling Practice's levels open one at a time; each needs a star on the one before.
  • Tip: Goliath's lines are in a deep, silly voice; it's fun to record your own.

🐺 The Good Shepherd

John 10: Jesus is the Good Shepherd who protects His sheep (and Ecclesiastes 4:12: a cord of three strands isn't easily broken).

A strategy game on an open meadow. Your flock grazes and wanders while thieves, wolves, lions, and bears come to bother it.

Sheep earn wool. Tap any patch of grass to place a helper there: fences, a crook shepherd who pulls strays back, a staff shepherd who holds trouble back, a sling shepherd who throws stones, or a sheepdog who walks a path you tap out.

Tap a helper to upgrade it. Sheep that stay close together are braver. Press ▶ to start each wave of trouble.

Nobody gets hurt: enough bonks and the trouble gets dizzy and runs off. Keep sheep safe to earn up to three stars; six levels open one at a time.

  • Little one: Glowing spots place a shepherd for free, tapping an enemy shoos it, waves start on their own, and lost sheep come home. It can't be lost.
  • Big kid: Spend wool, choose where helpers go, and start the waves yourself.
  • Challenge: Faster trouble, more of it, and each needs an extra bonk.
  • Tip: Start with a crook shepherd near the flock, and fences where the dirt trails come in.
  • Tip: A sheep that wanders off alone is the easiest one to lose.

🧱 Nehemiah's Wall

Nehemiah 1–6 and 12: Nehemiah leads God's people to rebuild Jerusalem's wall in 52 days, even when troublemakers try to stop them.

A lane strategy game. The broken wall is on the left; silly troublemakers walk in from the right along five lanes.

Tap stones to collect them. Spend them two ways: tap the wall to build it up a row, or place helpers on the squares (stone gatherers who dig up more stones, watchmen whose music bonks troublemakers, barricades, and more as you go). Troublemakers who reach the wall knock rows off.

Finish every lane's wall to win the gate. A trumpeter saves each lane once if trouble gets through, and Pray slows all the trouble down, once per gate. Nobody gets hurt: enough bonks and troublemakers get dizzy and go home.

Nine gates on a map of Jerusalem, with a big troublemaker at three of them, then a Day 52 celebration. An opening storybook tells how Nehemiah heard about the wall and asked the king to help.

  • Little one: Glowing spots place helpers for free, building the wall is free, stones collect themselves, tapping trouble shoos it, and the trumpeters never run out, so it can't be lost.
  • Big kid: Choose helpers from the cards along the top (each costs stones); each wall row takes a few seconds to build. Tapping a rubble heap or the king's cart shakes stones loose.
  • Challenge: Faster, tougher trouble and fewer stones to start; once there are more than six helpers, pick six before each gate.
  • Tip: Start with two or three stone gatherers, then put watchmen in front of them.
  • Tip: Build the wall when a lane is quiet, and keep some stones for repairs.

Recording your voice

Every line is read by a friendly narrator until you record it. In the grown-ups area, open Record your voice, pick who's recording, open a folder, and tap the red button to start and again to stop. Your recordings play first, on every iPad in your family.

Sharing with family

iPads signed in to the same Apple ID share children, grown-ups, and recordings automatically. For someone with a different Apple ID, like a grandparent, tap Invite family… in the grown-ups area and send the invitation. When they tap it on their iPad, they join your family.

Keeping a toddler in the game

Turn on Guided Access in Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access. Then triple-click the top (or home) button while a game is open. Triple-click again and enter your passcode to end it.

Troubleshooting

  • Recordings aren't showing up on another iPad: check that it's signed in to iCloud, then tap Sync now in the grown-ups area.
  • No sound: check the iPad's volume and the Sound section in the grown-ups area.
